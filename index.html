1. Long-Term Research Vision

The long-term goal is to develop expertise and leadership in designing safe, explainable, and robust robotic systems for real-world service and human-interaction scenarios across ground and marine robotic platforms, including Unmanned Surface Vehicles (USVs).

Higher studies (MTech / MTech by Research / PhD) are viewed as a capability-building and validation phase to establish strong foundations in perception, control, autonomous decision-making, kinematics, and dynamics.

A particular interest lies in unmanned surface vehicles (ground/marine), where reliable perception and safety-aware control are essential under real-world uncertainty. Prior experience in acoustic and vibro-acoustic sensing is viewed as a complementary strength for marine scenarios, where vision alone is often insufficient.

2. Core Engineering Philosophy

Robotic intelligence should be built as a structured hierarchy: Perception → Logic → Control. Learning-based methods are introduced only after deterministic perception, decision logic, and safety constraints are clearly defined. End-to-end black-box AI is deliberately avoided to ensure interpretability, stability, and safety.

3. High-Level System Architecture

Physical World → Perception → Logic (FSM) → Control → Actuation

4. Perception Layer (Structured Representation)

Raw sensor data is converted into explicit, interpretable variables. Perception does not make decisions; it only provides reliable facts with confidence estimates.

Example perception outputs (pseudo-code):

human_present: True / False
distance_to_human: value (cm)
attention_state: looking / not_looking / unknown
motion_state: static / slow / fast
confidence_score: 0 – 1

5. Logic Layer – Finite State Machines

High-level behavior is governed by deterministic logic using FSMs, ensuring predictable and explainable robot actions.

  • IDLE – monitoring state
  • AWARE – human or event detected
  • ENGAGED – safe interaction allowed
  • SAFE MODE – uncertainty, fault, or low confidence

6. Control & State Estimation (Core Strength)

  • Signal processing (time and frequency domain)
  • State-space modeling of coupled systems
  • Kalman Filter family (KF / EKF / UKF)
  • Classical control (PID, stability intuition)
  • Modern control (LQR / LQG)
  • Adaptive control (LMS / FXLMS)
Prior experience with FXLMS-based adaptive control provides strong intuition in closed-loop stability, disturbance rejection, and real-time implementation — directly relevant to robotics, marine systems, and safety-critical autonomy.
