1. Long-Term Engineering Ambition

The long-term goal is to develop expertise and leadership in designing safe, reliable, and intelligent autonomous robotic systems capable of operating across aerial, ground, and marine environments under real-world uncertainty.

Higher studies (MTech / MS by Research / PhD) are viewed as a capability-building and system-engineering phase for strengthening foundations in robotics, perception, autonomous systems, control, and intelligent decision architectures.

A central engineering interest lies in understanding how different sensing methodologies complement each other across environments. In aerial and ground robotics, vision alone is often insufficient, where LiDAR-based spatial perception improves environmental understanding. Similarly, in marine scenarios, prior experience in acoustic and vibro-acoustic systems is viewed as a complementary strength where conventional vision-based perception may become unreliable.

Current experience in FEM, structural dynamics, adaptive control, UAV-LiDAR systems, point cloud processing, and intelligent sensing is viewed as capability-building toward future multi-domain physical AI systems where perception, logic, AI, and control operate together as a unified engineering system.

2. Core Engineering Philosophy

Most robotic failures do not occur because a robot lacks features. They occur because the robot fails in: sensing reliability, decision consistency, system understanding, or safety-aware control .

The engineering philosophy therefore emphasizes: Perception → Logic → AI/ML → Planning → Control → Reliable Physical Interaction where AI acts as an enabling layer inside a stable and interpretable engineering system rather than replacing the system architecture itself.

Theoretical research is respected as the foundation of future technology, while personal engineering interests are primarily centered around the applied, implementation-oriented, and system-integration aspects of intelligent autonomous systems.

5. Logic, Safety & Autonomous Behaviour

High-level robotic behavior is governed through Finite State Machines (FSMs) and safety-aware decision architectures to ensure predictable, interpretable, and reliable system actions. FSMs provide structured behavioural control by defining how autonomous systems transition between operational states under different environmental conditions and safety constraints.

Example FSM States:

IDLE – monitoring state
AWARE – human or event detected
ENGAGED – safe interaction allowed
SAFE MODE – uncertainty, fault, or low confidence
  • Finite State Machines (FSM)

  • State estimation and probabilistic reasoning
